SECTION 1 — EXECUTIVE SUMMARY AND STRATEGIC CONTEXT

1.1 Purpose of This Document

This Statement of Work ("SOW") sets forth the terms, conditions, scope, deliverables, fees, and responsibilities under which Deloitte Consulting LLP ("Deloitte") will provide professional services to Waters Corporation ("Waters" or "Client") in connection with the AI Integration and Digital Transformation Program ("Program" or "the Engagement").

This SOW is entered into under and is governed by the Master Professional Services Agreement between Deloitte Consulting LLP and Waters Corporation dated January 15, 2026 ("MPSA"). In the event of a conflict between this SOW and the MPSA, the terms of the MPSA shall govern unless this SOW expressly and specifically states an intent to override a specific MPSA provision.

1.2 The Strategic Moment

Waters Corporation stands at an inflection point. The acquisition of certain Becton Dickinson Biosciences assets ("BD Assets") represents the largest and most complex transaction in Waters' corporate history. The combined entity must absorb approximately 8,500 additional employees, rationalize a combined application portfolio of over 1,200 systems, harmonize financial reporting across two distinct heritage environments, process an estimated 500 vendor contract novations, and exit all Transition Services Agreements ("TSAs") within 18 months — all while maintaining Waters' premium quality standards and delivering on a committed $290 million synergy target by Year 3.

These demands cannot be met at the pace or scale required through traditional manual approaches. Waters' Chief Information Officer, Brook Colangelo, has made a commitment to the Board of Directors that artificial intelligence will lead and accelerate this integration — not as an experiment or a pilot, but as the primary operating mechanism for the most demanding integration workstreams.

This Program is the first and most critical step in that commitment.

1.3 Deloitte's Perspective on the Opportunity

Deloitte has delivered AI-led integration programs for seven life sciences and industrial technology companies over the past three years, including two programs of comparable scale and complexity. From this experience, we have identified three characteristics that determine whether AI-led integrations succeed or fail at the speed required:

First, the quality and speed of the first AI win. Organizations that demonstrate a measurable AI result within the first four weeks build the organizational confidence and executive momentum required to sustain the program through the harder, more complex workstreams that follow. Programs that spend the first 60 to 90 days in strategy and governance design without a visible outcome typically stall.

Second, the presence of a shared AI platform and operating model before scale. Organizations that allow individual workstreams to procure and deploy AI tools independently create technical debt, governance gaps, security vulnerabilities, and integration complexity that ultimately slows the program. A shared AI Engine — a common platform, governance framework, and operating model — is not overhead; it is the infrastructure that makes speed possible.

Third, the integration of change management into technical delivery from day one. AI deployments that treat change management as a downstream activity consistently underperform. Users who do not understand what the AI is doing, cannot see how it benefits them, and do not trust its outputs will find ways to work around it. AI that is technically correct but organizationally unused delivers no value.

Deloitte's approach to this Program is designed around these three principles. We begin with a visible, measurable first win in Week 2. We build the shared AI Engine in parallel. And we embed change management at every step of the technical delivery.

SECTION 2 — BACKGROUND AND BUSINESS CONTEXT

2.1 Waters Corporation — Company Overview

Waters Corporation (NYSE: WAT) was founded in 1958 and is headquartered in Milford, Massachusetts. Waters is a global leader in analytical instruments, software, and services for laboratory and industrial applications. The company serves customers in pharmaceutical, life sciences, food safety, environmental, and chemical industries across more than 100 countries.

Key financial and operational metrics:
- Annual revenue: approximately $3.1 billion (FY2025)
- Number of employees (legacy Waters): approximately 7,500
- Global office and laboratory locations: 58
- Key product lines: HPLC and UPLC systems, mass spectrometry, laboratory informatics, software services
- Key geographies: North America (38%), Europe (32%), Asia Pacific (24%), Rest of World (6%)
- R&D investment: approximately $220 million annually
- Number of active software systems (legacy Waters): approximately 520

2.2 The Becton Dickinson Asset Acquisition

On October 31, 2025, Waters completed the acquisition of certain Biosciences and Diagnostic Solutions assets from Becton Dickinson and Company ("BD") for a total consideration of approximately $17.1 billion. The acquired assets comprise BD's flow cytometry, cell sorting, genomics, and laboratory diagnostics businesses.

The acquisition significantly expands Waters' addressable market, adding capabilities in cellular biology, genomics, and diagnostics to Waters' existing strength in analytical chemistry and mass spectrometry. The combined entity will be a comprehensive life sciences tools company serving the full spectrum of drug discovery, development, and diagnostic applications.

Acquired asset profile:
- BD employees joining Waters: approximately 8,500 (across 34 countries)
- BD facilities: 22 manufacturing sites, 67 office locations
- BD software applications in scope: approximately 680 (of which approximately 520 are candidates for rationalization)
- BD vendor contracts requiring novation: approximately 480
- BD annual IT spend: approximately $340 million
- BD ERP platform: SAP ECC 6.0 (migration to S/4HANA planned for 2027)
- BD CRM platform: Salesforce
- BD HR platform: Workday (Waters uses SuccessFactors — platform consolidation TBD)

2.3 Integration Complexity and Urgency

The integration presents challenges of exceptional complexity across multiple dimensions:

TSA Obligations and Timeline Pressure
Waters has entered into Transition Services Agreements with BD for a range of shared services including IT infrastructure, finance, HR, and supply chain. TSA fees are significant — estimated at $28 million per month — and Waters is contractually obligated to exit all TSAs within 18 months of closing (by April 30, 2027). Each month of delay in TSA exit represents approximately $28 million in avoidable cost. The financial pressure to exit TSAs on time is severe.

Application Rationalization at Unprecedented Scale
The combined application portfolio of approximately 1,200 systems must be reduced to approximately 500 systems through a kill/keep/clone/retire analysis. Each application retirement requires vendor contract novation or termination, dependency mapping, data migration, user transition, and decommissioning. At the current manual pace, the application rationalization team estimates it takes approximately 8 weeks to complete one full analysis cycle across the active portfolio. This pace is incompatible with TSA exit timelines.

Contract Novation Volume
Approximately 480 BD vendor contracts must be formally novated — transferred from BD to Waters — as part of the integration. The novation process for each contract involves legal review, vendor notification, bilateral execution, change impact analysis, and system updates. The procurement and legal teams are not scaled to process this volume within the required timeframe using manual methods.

Financial Reporting Fragmentation
The combined entity has two distinct heritage financial environments. Waters uses Oracle Financials with a Waters-specific chart of accounts, SKU hierarchy, and revenue recognition methodology. BD uses SAP ECC 6.0 with entirely different structures. The CFO and SLT currently have no consolidated view of the combined entity's financial performance. Investors and analysts are tracking a $290 million revenue synergy commitment that requires clean, consolidated reporting to validate.

Dual HR Systems and Employee Experience
The combined workforce of approximately 16,000 employees is navigating two HR systems (SuccessFactors and Workday), two benefits structures, two payroll systems, two sets of HR policies, and two organizational hierarchies. The HR service desk is experiencing a surge in tickets from employees seeking guidance on benefits, payroll transitions, org reporting, and policy changes. The HR team does not have capacity to handle this volume manually.

2.4 The AI Imperative

Waters CIO Brook Colangelo has made a public commitment, supported by the Board of Directors, that AI will be the primary mechanism for accelerating the integration. This commitment reflects a recognition that traditional integration approaches — which rely on headcount scaling and manual process — cannot deliver the speed and scale required within the TSA timeline.

The commitment to AI also reflects an opportunity that goes beyond the integration itself. The discipline, tooling, and organizational capability built during the integration can become the foundation of a permanent AI-native operating model for the combined Waters. The integration is the forcing function that creates the conditions — urgency, data, organizational motivation, executive mandate — that make rapid AI adoption possible.

Waters has made initial platform decisions:
- Selected Anthropic Claude as the primary LLM for enterprise AI deployment
- Security review of Claude API deployment completed and approved by CISO
- Initial AI Guild established under the leadership of Hari Patel, Director of Digital Innovation
- Microsoft Azure selected as primary cloud infrastructure platform (with existing enterprise agreement)

These decisions create a strong starting point. The challenge is moving from platform selection and early guild experimentation to production-grade AI deployment at the scale and pace the integration requires.

Deloitte has an established understanding of Waters' technology landscape, governance culture, and key stakeholder relationships.

SECTION 4 — SCOPE OF SERVICES — DETAILED

4.1 Workstream 1 — AI Strategy and Governance Design

4.1.1 Overview and Rationale
The AI Strategy and Governance workstream establishes the foundation upon which all other workstreams operate. Without a clear AI strategy, a functioning governance model, and a shared understanding of the AI platform architecture, individual workstreams will make inconsistent decisions, create technical debt, and generate governance gaps that create risk. This workstream runs in parallel with early delivery workstreams and must be substantially complete by end of Month 4 to enable scaling.

4.1.2 AI Maturity Assessment
Deloitte will conduct a comprehensive assessment of Waters' current AI maturity across both legacy Waters and BD, covering the following dimensions:

Strategy and Vision: Clarity of AI strategy, executive alignment, investment commitment, and link to business objectives.

Data Readiness: Quality, accessibility, governance, and documentation of the data assets required to support AI use cases across the integration.

Technology and Platform: Current AI tools and platforms in use across the organization, cloud infrastructure readiness, API and integration capabilities, and security posture.

Talent and Organization: Current AI skills across the Waters AI Guild, IT organization, and business units. Gap analysis versus requirements for identified use cases.

Governance and Risk: Existing AI governance mechanisms, risk frameworks, ethical AI policies, and compliance requirements relevant to life sciences.

Culture and Adoption: Organizational readiness for AI adoption, level of leadership sponsorship, employee sentiment, and historical patterns of technology adoption.

The maturity assessment will be conducted through:
- 35 to 45 structured stakeholder interviews (Waters and BD personnel) using Deloitte's standardized AI Maturity Interview Protocol
- Review of existing documentation including IT architecture documents, data governance policies, AI-related vendor contracts, and current AI tool usage
- Technical architecture review sessions with Waters IT Architecture team (minimum 4 sessions)
- Data landscape review sessions with Waters Data and Analytics team (minimum 3 sessions)
- Survey of approximately 200 Waters and BD employees across business functions to assess AI sentiment and readiness

Output: AI Maturity Assessment Report (D-001) and accompanying AI Opportunity Map (D-001a)

4.1.3 AI Governance Framework Design
Deloitte will design and document a comprehensive AI governance framework for Waters covering:

AI Use Case Governance: Process for identifying, evaluating, prioritizing, and approving AI use cases including risk assessment criteria, data readiness requirements, ethical review, and business case thresholds.

AI Model Governance: Standards for model selection, fine-tuning approval, prompt governance, output monitoring, hallucination auditing, and model versioning. Specific attention to large language model governance given Waters' Claude deployment.

AI Data Governance: Standards for data quality, data classification, data ingestion into AI systems, PII handling, and data retention for AI-processed content.

AI Vendor and Tooling Governance: Decision framework for selecting AI vendors and tools. Policy on AI tool proliferation, shadow AI, and employee use of consumer AI tools (e.g., ChatGPT, Copilot). Standards for evaluating and onboarding new AI tools.

AI Risk and Compliance: Risk taxonomy for AI-related risks including hallucination risk, bias risk, data privacy risk, security risk, and regulatory risk. Escalation and incident management procedures for AI failures. Compliance mapping to relevant regulations including GDPR, CCPA, and FDA guidance on AI in life sciences.

AI Ethics Policy: Ethical principles for AI use at Waters, including transparency requirements, human oversight standards, prohibited use cases, and accountability framework.

The framework will be designed through a series of governance design workshops with Waters stakeholders:
- Workshop 1: AI risk and compliance requirements (Waters Legal, Compliance, and IT Security)
- Workshop 2: AI use case governance and prioritization framework (Waters CIO, PMO, and Business Unit leads)
- Workshop 3: AI model and data governance standards (Waters IT Architecture and Data teams)
- Workshop 4: AI ethics policy and organizational accountability (Waters CIO, CHRO, and Legal)

Output: AI Governance Framework (D-002), AI Ethics Policy (D-002a), AI Risk Register (D-002b)

4.1.4 AI Vendor and Tooling Recommendation
Building on Waters' initial platform decisions (Claude as primary LLM, Azure as cloud infrastructure), Deloitte will conduct a structured evaluation of the additional AI tooling required to deliver the program, including:

Orchestration and Agent Frameworks: Evaluation of leading agent orchestration frameworks (LangChain, AutoGen, CrewAI, custom) against Waters' requirements for the integration use cases.

Vector Database and Retrieval Infrastructure: Evaluation of vector database options (Azure AI Search, Pinecone, Weaviate, Chroma) for the RAG architecture required across contract and HR use cases.

Document Ingestion and Processing: Evaluation of document processing pipelines for large-scale contract and policy document ingestion (Azure Document Intelligence, Unstructured.io, custom).

AI Monitoring and Observability: Evaluation of AI output monitoring and observability tools (Arize, Weights and Biases, LangSmith, Datadog AI monitoring) for production governance.

Workflow and Human-in-the-Loop: Evaluation of workflow orchestration tools for human review and approval workflows (Power Automate, Camunda, custom) compatible with Waters' existing Microsoft environment.

The evaluation will be structured as a weighted scoring matrix against criteria defined with Waters, covering: technical capability, security and compliance, cost, vendor stability, integration complexity, Waters existing licensing, and alignment with Claude/Azure ecosystem.

Output: AI Vendor and Tooling Recommendation Report (D-003), including implementation roadmap and total cost of ownership analysis for recommended stack.

4.1.5 AI Center of Excellence Design
Deloitte will design the operating model for Waters' AI Center of Excellence ("AI CoE"), which will serve as the permanent home for Waters' AI capability beyond the integration program. The AI CoE design will cover:

Mission and Scope: Definition of the AI CoE's mandate, scope of authority, and relationship to business units and IT.

Organizational Structure: Recommended org structure including leadership roles (AI CoE Director, AI Platform Lead, AI Governance Lead, AI Product Managers, AI Engineers, Data Engineers, Change Management Lead). Role profiles for each position.

Talent Strategy: Assessment of current Waters AI Guild capability versus AI CoE requirements. Recommendations for internal talent development, external hiring, and strategic partnerships. Training curriculum for AI Guild members.

Operating Model: How the AI CoE will interact with business units to identify, prioritize, fund, and deliver AI use cases. Budget model for AI CoE operations. Demand management process.

Platform Ownership: AI CoE's role in managing the shared AI Engine platform, including vendor relationships, API key management, cost governance, and security.

Metrics and Value Tracking: How the AI CoE will measure and report on AI program performance including use case throughput, value realized, adoption metrics, and governance compliance.

Transition from Engagement: Plan for transferring Deloitte-supported capabilities to Waters' internal AI CoE over the course of Phase 2 and Phase 3.

Output: AI Center of Excellence Design (D-004), including organizational blueprint, role profiles, and 12-month standup roadmap.

4.2 Workstream 2 — Contract Lifecycle Management AI Program

4.2.1 Overview and Business Problem
The Contract Lifecycle Management workstream addresses one of the most acute and time-sensitive integration challenges Waters faces. Approximately 480 BD vendor contracts must be novated — formally transferred from BD to Waters — as part of the integration. Additionally, Waters' own procurement function is managing an elevated volume of new vendor contracts and contract renewals driven by the integration.

The current state is manual and email-based. There is no contract lifecycle management (CLM) system in place. Contracts are stored across SharePoint folders, email attachments, and local drives. There is no systematic process for tracking contract status, novation progress, or downstream system dependencies. Legal review is manual and operates as a bottleneck. Procurement cycle times currently range from 4 to 16 weeks per contract depending on complexity.

The financial stakes of this situation are direct: vendor contract novations that are delayed create TSA exit risks, because applications cannot be decommissioned until their underlying vendor contracts are resolved. Every week of delay in the novation process is a potential week of TSA fee exposure.

4.2.2 CLM Current State Assessment
Deloitte will conduct a comprehensive current state assessment of Waters' contract management processes, covering:

Process Mapping: Detailed process maps for the full contract lifecycle as currently practiced at Waters and BD, including contract initiation, review, approval, execution, storage, renewal management, and termination. Swim-lane diagrams showing the roles of Procurement, Legal, IT, Finance, and Business Unit stakeholders.

Contract Repository Inventory: Inventory of where contracts are currently stored across both Waters and BD, including SharePoint sites, email archives, shared drives, and any partial CLM tooling. Assessment of document completeness, format distribution (PDF, DOCX, scanned images), and language distribution.

Volume and Complexity Analysis: Analysis of the approximately 480 BD contracts requiring novation, categorized by contract type (MSA, SOW, SaaS subscription, maintenance agreement, data processing agreement), value tier, vendor relationship complexity, and novation urgency based on TSA exit dependency.

Stakeholder and Capacity Analysis: Assessment of current staffing in Procurement and Legal. Number of FTEs dedicated to contract management, current throughput (contracts processed per week), capacity constraints, and skill gaps.

System and Integration Landscape: Inventory of systems that have dependencies on vendor contracts, including the CMDB, Ariba, Oracle Financials, and any other procurement or vendor management systems.

Pain Point and Opportunity Interviews: Structured interviews with 12 to 15 stakeholders across Procurement, Legal, IT, and Finance to capture specific pain points, manual effort hotspots, and AI opportunity areas.

Output: CLM Current State Assessment Report (D-005), including process maps, contract volume analysis, capacity analysis, and prioritized opportunity areas.

4.2.3 CLM AI Requirements Specification
Based on the current state assessment, Deloitte will develop a detailed requirements specification for the AI-assisted contract management system, covering:

Functional Requirements:
- FR-001: System shall ingest contracts in PDF, DOCX, and scanned image formats (minimum)
- FR-002: System shall extract a defined set of metadata fields from each contract (field set defined in requirements specification based on D-005 findings)
- FR-003: System shall compare extracted metadata against a configurable Waters enterprise contract requirements checklist
- FR-004: System shall flag non-compliant, missing, or ambiguous clauses with risk classification (High/Medium/Low)
- FR-005: System shall generate first-draft novation documents based on extracted contract metadata and configurable templates
- FR-006: System shall generate vendor communication drafts for novation notification
- FR-007: System shall perform change impact analysis flagging downstream systems dependent on each vendor contract
- FR-008: System shall provide a dashboard showing novation pipeline status, processing progress, cycle time metrics, and TSA exit dependency tracking
- FR-009: System shall support human review and approval workflow before any AI-generated document is sent to a vendor
- FR-010: System shall maintain a full audit log of all AI actions, human reviews, approvals, and document versions
- FR-011: System shall support multi-user access with role-based permissions (Procurement, Legal, IT, Read-Only)
- FR-012: System shall integrate with Waters' document storage (SharePoint) for contract repository management

Non-Functional Requirements:
- NFR-001: System shall process a standard contract (up to 50 pages) within 60 seconds
- NFR-002: System shall support concurrent processing of at least 20 contracts simultaneously
- NFR-003: System shall achieve metadata extraction accuracy of at least 90% on a defined validation set
- NFR-004: System shall maintain 99.5% uptime during business hours
- NFR-005: All contract data shall be encrypted at rest and in transit
- NFR-006: System shall be deployed within Waters' security boundary (Azure private deployment)
- NFR-007: System shall not transmit contract data to any third-party service other than the approved LLM API
- NFR-008: Audit logs shall be retained for a minimum of 7 years

Output: CLM AI Requirements Specification (D-006), including functional requirements, non-functional requirements, user stories, and acceptance criteria for each requirement.

4.2.4 Contract Metadata Extraction Architecture Design
Deloitte will design the technical architecture for the contract metadata extraction and AI review system, including:

Data Ingestion Pipeline: Design of the document ingestion pipeline including file format handling, OCR processing for scanned documents, text extraction quality validation, and chunking strategy for large documents.

Extraction Schema Design: Definition of the contract metadata schema — the specific fields to be extracted from each contract. Based on Waters' requirements and Deloitte's experience across comparable engagements, the initial schema will target approximately 150 metadata fields covering:
- Contract identification fields (parties, dates, reference numbers, contract type)
- Commercial terms (total value, payment terms, pricing structure, expense policy)
- Obligation fields (key obligations of each party, deliverable schedules, milestone dates)

4.3 Workstream 3 — Financial Reporting Harmonization

4.3.1 Overview and Business Problem
The combined Waters and BD entity presents one of the most complex financial reporting integration challenges Deloitte has encountered in a comparable transaction. The two legacy environments use entirely different financial systems (Oracle Financials vs. SAP ECC 6.0), different charts of accounts, different SKU hierarchies, different revenue recognition methodologies, and different close cycle processes.

The CFO, SLT, and investor relations team are operating four months post-close without a single consolidated view of the combined entity's financial performance. Revenue synergy commitments of $290 million by Year 3 cannot be credibly tracked without harmonized reporting. Wall Street analysts are increasingly focused on integration execution, and clean consolidated reporting is essential to maintaining investor confidence.

This workstream does not attempt to resolve the full ERP consolidation — that is a multi-year program tied to the SAP S/4HANA migration. Instead, it creates an AI-mediated reconciliation and reporting layer that generates consolidated views from the existing heritage systems, buying the organization the clean reporting it needs while the deeper infrastructure work proceeds.

4.3.2 Financial Reporting Current State Assessment
Deloitte will conduct a comprehensive current state assessment of financial reporting across both legacy entities:

Close Process Mapping: Detailed process maps of the current monthly and quarterly close process for both legacy Waters (Oracle) and legacy BD (SAP), including timeline, key activities, responsible roles, system touch points, and known bottlenecks.

Chart of Accounts Comparison: Side-by-side analysis of the Waters and BD charts of accounts, identifying structural differences, equivalent accounts, unmatched accounts, and the complexity of mapping between them.

SKU Hierarchy Analysis: Analysis of the Waters and BD product hierarchies at the SKU level, including total SKU count per entity, hierarchy depth, naming conventions, classification logic, and estimated overlap. Initial estimates suggest 15,000 to 25,000 SKUs across the combined entity with significant mapping complexity.

Revenue Recognition Comparison: Comparison of revenue recognition policies and methodologies across Waters and BD, including any differences in timing, bundling, variable consideration treatment, and geography-specific rules.

Reporting Requirements Mapping: Interviews with Waters CFO office, FP&A team, and investor relations to document the specific consolidated reporting outputs required, including frequency, granularity, and audience.

Close Cycle Bottleneck Analysis: Identification of the specific activities in the close process that are consuming the most time and that are most amenable to AI acceleration.

Output: Financial Reporting Current State Assessment (D-011), including process maps, chart of accounts comparison, SKU hierarchy analysis, and reporting requirements map.

4.3.3 SKU Mapping and Reconciliation Design
Based on the current state assessment, Deloitte will design the AI-assisted SKU mapping and reconciliation approach:

SKU Mapping Methodology: Design of the methodology for mapping BD SKUs to Waters product hierarchy equivalents using large language model analysis of SKU descriptions, product attributes, and historical sales patterns. This will include: (a) AI-generated mapping proposals with confidence scores; (b) Human review and validation workflow for low-confidence mappings; (c) Mapping governance process for ongoing maintenance.

Reconciliation Architecture: Design of the automated reconciliation approach for aligning booking events from the two heritage systems, including: transaction matching logic, anomaly detection rules for unmatched or inconsistent transactions, ML-based gap-filling for missing data, and audit trail requirements.

Consolidated Reporting Architecture: Design of the consolidated reporting layer, including data aggregation logic, dimension harmonization, period alignment, and output format specifications for SLT dashboard, investor reporting, and FP&A workbench.

Output: SKU Mapping Analysis and Reconciliation Design (D-012), including mapping methodology, reconciliation architecture, and consolidated reporting specifications.

4.3.4 AI-Assisted Financial Reporting Tool Configuration
Based on the approved design (D-012), Deloitte will configure the AI-assisted financial reporting layer:

SKU Mapping Engine: Configuration of the LLM-based SKU mapping engine against the Waters and BD product catalogs. Initial batch processing of all SKUs to generate mapping proposals. Human review interface for validation.

Reconciliation Engine: Configuration of the automated reconciliation engine for booking-to-revenue alignment. Integration with Oracle Financials and SAP ECC 6.0 data extracts (read-only access).

Generative Reporting: Configuration of AI-powered narrative generation for management reporting, including BU-level financial summaries, variance commentary, and executive dashboard narratives.

Pilot BU Deployment: The first deployment will cover a single, selected business unit where data quality and stakeholder availability are highest. Lessons learned from the pilot will inform the approach for remaining BUs.

Output: Configured financial reporting AI layer for pilot BU (D-013), including system documentation and pilot BU sign-off.

4.3.5 Close Cycle Compression Roadmap
Deloitte will develop a roadmap for compressing the financial close cycle through AI and automation, covering:

Quick Win Opportunities: AI automation opportunities that can reduce close cycle time within 90 days.

Medium-Term Opportunities: Process redesign and AI integration opportunities for 6-12 month timeline.

S/4HANA Integration Path: How the AI reporting layer will connect to and eventually be superseded by the Waters S/4HANA consolidation (planned 2027). Ensuring the AI layer does not create technical debt for the S/4HANA migration.

Output: Close Cycle Compression Roadmap (D-014), including initiative prioritization, timeline, resource requirements, and S/4HANA alignment plan.

4.4 Workstream 4 — Application Rationalization Advisory

4.4.1 Overview and Business Problem
The combined Waters and BD application portfolio contains approximately 1,200 systems. Waters' target state is approximately 500 systems — a rationalization of approximately 700 applications over the next 3 to 5 years. However, the TSA exit obligation creates near-term urgency: applications that are hosted on BD infrastructure or licensed under BD agreements must be addressed within the 18-month TSA window.

Portfolio analysis has been completed at a high level. The kill/keep/clone/retire decisions are approximately 60% complete. The challenge is execution: the per-application execution work — dependency mapping, vendor contract review, migration planning, stakeholder notification, decommissioning — is done entirely manually and is not progressing at the pace required by TSA timelines.

This workstream is advisory: Deloitte will design the AI-assisted rationalization approach and framework but will not build production AI systems for application rationalization in Phase 1. Full production build for this workstream is planned for Phase 2.

4.4.2 Application Portfolio Assessment
Deloitte will conduct an advisory assessment of the current application rationalization effort:

Portfolio Status Review: Review of the current kill/keep/clone/retire analysis status, including which applications have decisions, which are pending, and which are blocked.

TSA Exit Dependency Mapping: Identification of which application decisions are directly on the critical path to TSA exits, and the current risk to TSA exit timelines based on rationalization pace.

Manual Process Analysis: Analysis of the current manual application rationalization process, including per-application effort estimates, bottleneck identification, and quality of current outputs.

AI Opportunity Identification: Identification of specific activities within the rationalization process where AI can provide the greatest acceleration.

CMDB Assessment: Assessment of the current state of the CMDB (Configuration Management Database), including completeness, accuracy, and integration with ServiceNow discovery.

Output: Application Portfolio Assessment Advisory Report (D-015), including portfolio status, TSA risk analysis, and AI opportunity map.

4.4.3 AI-Augmented Rationalization Framework Design
Deloitte will design the framework for AI-assisted application rationalization:

AI Kill/Keep/Clone/Retire Analysis: Design of the AI-assisted decision support approach for rationalization decisions, including: what data feeds the AI analysis (CMDB, usage telemetry, vendor contract data, business stakeholder inputs), what AI does (scores applications against criteria, identifies dependencies, flags risks), and how human decision-makers use AI outputs.

Dependency Mapping Approach: Design of AI-assisted dependency mapping using CMDB data, API telemetry, source code analysis, and documentation analysis to build application dependency graphs.

Contract-to-Application Linkage: Design of the integration between the CLM AI system (Workstream 2) and the application rationalization pipeline, enabling automated identification of vendor contracts associated with each application to be retired.

Execution Pipeline Design: Design of the AI-orchestrated execution pipeline for application retirement, including automated notification drafting, change management workflow, decommissioning checklist, and audit documentation.

Output: AI-Augmented Application Rationalization Framework (D-016), including framework design, data requirements, integration architecture, and Phase 2 implementation roadmap.

Training design and delivery: Overseeing the development and delivery of training programs across all workstreams. Maintaining a training calendar. Tracking completion rates.

SECTION 6 — PROJECT METHODOLOGY

6.1 Deloitte Accelerate AI™ Methodology

Deloitte will deliver this engagement using its proprietary Accelerate AI™ methodology, which has been developed and refined across 47 enterprise AI deployments over the past three years. The methodology is structured around four phases with specific activities, tools, and outputs at each phase.

6.1.1 Phase A — Discover (Months 1-2)

Objectives: Establish ground truth about Waters' current state, AI maturity, data landscape, and integration complexity. Identify and prioritize AI use cases. Establish program governance.

Key Activities:
- Stakeholder interview program (35-45 interviews across Waters and BD)
- Current state documentation review (contracts, HR policies, financial reports, application inventory, architecture documents)
- Data landscape assessment (identifying where the data lives for each use case)
- AI maturity assessment (six-dimension framework)
- Use case identification and prioritization workshops
- Program governance establishment (charter, RACI, cadences)
- AI Engine setup: team onboarding, platform access, tool procurement

Tools: Deloitte AI Maturity Assessment Protocol, Use Case Prioritization Matrix, Data Readiness Scorecard, Stakeholder Interview Guide Library (17 role-specific guides)

Phase Gate: Discovery Phase Gate Review at end of Month 2. Waters Program Sponsor must approve AI Maturity Assessment (D-001) and CLM Current State Assessment (D-005) before Phase B begins.

6.1.2 Phase B — Design (Months 3-4)

Objectives: Design the AI systems, governance framework, and operating model. Make technology and architectural decisions. Finalize requirements. Begin building organizational readiness.

Key Activities:
- AI Governance Framework design and stakeholder workshops
- CLM requirements specification finalization
- Contract metadata extraction architecture design and security review
- Financial reporting reconciliation design
- Application rationalization framework design
- Technology selection for full stack
- Prompt engineering research and experimentation (pre-build phase)
- Change management stakeholder analysis and communication planning
- Training curriculum design

Tools: Deloitte Architecture Decision Record Template, Technology Evaluation Scorecard, Prompt Engineering Playbook, Change Impact Assessment Tool

Phase Gate: Design Phase Gate Review at end of Month 4. Waters IT Architecture Lead must approve architecture design (D-007) before Phase C begins. Waters Program Sponsor must approve AI Governance Framework (D-002).

6.1.3 Phase C — Build and Validate (Months 5-8)

Objectives: Build, configure, test, and validate the AI systems across all workstreams. Run parallel change management and training activities. Demonstrate measurable AI value.

- Severity 4 (Low): Minor issue with no functional impact. Resolution target: Sprint backlog.

SECTION 11 — RISKS AND MITIGATION STRATEGIES

11.1 Program Risk Register

RISK-001: BD Contract Repository Delayed or Incomplete
Probability: High | Impact: High | Overall: CRITICAL
Description: The BD contract repository may not be available in digital, machine-readable format within the required timeframe. BD contracts may be stored in formats (scanned paper, legacy systems) that require significant preprocessing before AI can process them.
Mitigation: (a) Deloitte CLM team to conduct repository readiness assessment in Week 1; (b) Waters PMO Lead to escalate BD data availability to ESC by end of Week 2; (c) If repository not available by end of Month 1, initiate change order discussion.
Contingency: If BD contracts are not available by Month 2, the CLM workstream will pivot to focus on Waters' own procurement contracts (new MSAs, SOWs) as the first production use case, with BD novation contracts addressed once available.

RISK-002: Waters Stakeholder Availability
Probability: High | Impact: High | Overall: HIGH
Description: The Waters program team (PMO Lead, workstream leads, subject matter experts) are simultaneously managing their day jobs and the BD integration. Time commitment to this program will compete with other urgent demands.
Mitigation: (a) Deloitte to maximize asynchronous collaboration and minimize time burden of program participation; (b) All Waters stakeholder time requests submitted minimum 5 business days in advance; (c) Weekly check-in with Waters Program Sponsor to identify availability constraints early; (d) Design all workshops for maximum efficiency — no more than 2 hours per session, pre-read materials distributed minimum 2 days in advance.
Contingency: If a critical Waters resource becomes unavailable for more than 2 weeks, Deloitte to propose interim coverage approach to PMC.

RISK-003: Extraction Accuracy Below Threshold
Probability: Medium | Impact: High | Overall: HIGH
Description: The contract metadata extraction accuracy may not achieve the required 90% threshold in UAT, particularly for complex contracts with non-standard structures, scanned documents, or non-English language content.
Mitigation: (a) Conduct prompt engineering research and optimization in Month 4 before build commences; (b) Build accuracy measurement into each sprint review; (c) Establish human review workflow that catches extraction errors before they propagate; (d) Set progressive accuracy targets by sprint rather than a single end-of-build target.
Contingency: If accuracy targets are not met by Sprint 7 (November), initiate change order discussion for additional prompt engineering sprints or scope reduction.

RISK-004: Scope Creep from Integration Complexity
Probability: High | Impact: Medium | Overall: HIGH
Description: The Waters integration is a dynamic environment. New requirements, new use cases, and expanding stakeholder requests are likely as the program progresses and the AI value becomes visible.
Mitigation: (a) Rigorous change order discipline — no scope additions without written change order; (b) Maintain a published parking lot for out-of-scope requests; (c) Deloitte Engagement Manager to review all Waters stakeholder requests for scope alignment before committing; (d) Monthly scope health review at PMC.
Contingency: If accumulated scope change requests exceed $500,000, convene ESC for strategic prioritization discussion.

RISK-005: AI Platform Evolution Disrupting Build
Probability: Medium | Impact: Medium | Overall: MEDIUM
Description: Anthropic may release significant Claude updates during the build phase that alter API behavior, pricing, or capability in ways that affect the program.
Mitigation: (a) Architecture team to monitor Anthropic release notes weekly; (b) All API integrations built with version pinning to prevent unplanned updates; (c) Model version transitions planned and tested before deployment.
Contingency: Evaluate impact of any major Claude update within 5 business days of release; initiate change order if update requires significant rework.

RISK-006: Data Quality Issues in Financial Reporting Workstream
Probability: High | Impact: Medium | Overall: HIGH
Description: Financial data from the BD SAP environment may have quality issues (missing fields, inconsistent classifications, data entry errors) that prevent AI reconciliation from achieving the required accuracy.
Mitigation: (a) Data quality assessment conducted as part of D-011; (b) Data quality thresholds defined before AI build commences; (c) AI reconciliation designed to flag rather than auto-correct uncertain mappings.
Contingency: If data quality is below threshold for AI processing, scope AI reconciliation to human-assisted rather than automated mode.

EXHIBIT A — EXPENSE REIMBURSEMENT POLICY

A.1 General Policy
Deloitte will be reimbursed for reasonable and documented out-of-pocket expenses directly related to the performance of Services under this SOW. All expenses must be submitted with original receipts and submitted monthly with invoices.

A.2 Air Travel
Domestic flights under 4 hours: economy class only
Domestic flights over 4 hours: economy class (business class requires prior written approval from Waters Program Sponsor)
International flights: business class permitted
Advance booking: minimum 7 days in advance where operationally practical
Airline status upgrades using personal points/miles are not reimbursable

A.3 Ground Transportation
Rental car: permitted when public transport is not reasonably available; standard class vehicle only
Ride-share and taxi: permitted; receipts required for amounts over $25
Personal vehicle mileage: reimbursed at current IRS standard mileage rate
Parking: reimbursed with receipt

A.4 Hotel Accommodation
Major metropolitan areas (New York, Boston, San Francisco, Los Angeles, Chicago, Washington DC): not to exceed $350 per night
Other US locations: not to exceed $275 per night
International: not to exceed $300 per night (local currency equivalent)
Hotel loyalty points and upgrades using personal points are not reimbursable; base room rate only

A.5 Meals
Daily per diem: $75 per person per day (receipts required for amounts over $25)
Business meals with Waters personnel: up to $100 per person with prior written approval from Waters Program Sponsor
Alcohol: not reimbursable unless included in a business meal approved by Waters Program Sponsor

A.6 Client Entertainment
Client entertainment (events, activities, sporting events, performances): requires prior written approval from Waters Program Sponsor; not to exceed $150 per person per event; alcohol costs not separately reimbursable

A.7 Technology and Communications
Mobile phone roaming charges: reimbursed with documentation
Printing and document production directly related to engagement deliverables: reimbursed with receipt
Software or technology purchases above $500: require prior written approval from Waters Program Sponsor

A.8 Expenses Not Reimbursable
Personal expenses of any kind; traffic fines, parking tickets, or similar violations; first-class air travel without prior written approval; hotel amenity fees (minibar, room service, spa, gym) not pre-approved; personal entertainment

EXHIBIT B — DELIVERABLE ACCEPTANCE PROCESS

B.1 Delivery Notification
Deloitte shall notify Waters in writing (email acceptable) when a deliverable is ready for review. Notification shall include: deliverable reference number, deliverable title, delivery date, and review deadline.

B.2 Review Period
Waters shall review each deliverable within the timeframe specified in Section 5 for that deliverable (typically 10 or 15 business days).

B.3 Feedback Process
Waters shall provide feedback using the standard feedback template (available from Deloitte Engagement Manager). Feedback shall categorize comments as: (a) Critical — must be resolved before acceptance; (b) Important — should be resolved in revised version; (c) Minor — optional for acceptance.

B.4 Revision
Deloitte shall provide a revised version addressing Waters' Critical and Important comments within 5 business days of receiving Waters' feedback.

B.5 Acceptance
Acceptance shall be confirmed by Waters in writing (email from Waters Program Sponsor acceptable). A deliverable shall be deemed accepted if: (a) Waters provides written acceptance; or (b) Waters does not provide feedback within the specified review period.

B.6 Disputed Acceptance
If Waters rejects a revised deliverable, the dispute shall be escalated to the Program Management Committee for resolution.

EXHIBIT F — CHANGE ORDER PROCESS AND TEMPLATE

F.1 Change Order Process
Step 1: Either party identifies a proposed change to scope, timeline, fees, or resources.
Step 2: Requesting party documents the proposed change using the Change Order Request template below.
Step 3: Deloitte Engagement Manager assesses impact on scope, timeline, and fees within 5 business days.
Step 4: Deloitte presents Change Order for Waters review and approval per approval thresholds in Section 7.3.
Step 5: Upon written approval by both parties, change is incorporated into the program.
Step 6: No change is binding until the Change Order is signed by both parties.
